Try a policy before enforcing it

A policy is easy to read and hard to predict. Whether Enforce would refuse anything in your cluster depends on your images, your scan results and your triage decisions — not on the policy text.

That is why installations sit in Audit for months: the operator cannot see what switching would cost, so they never switch. The replay answers it from the cluster's own records. Every admission review is already in the ledger; a draft policy is decided against those rows again, by the same code the admission webhook runs, and the differences are reported before anything is saved.

Nothing is applied. No pod is affected, no scan is queued, no registry is contacted, and the licence meter is not touched. What it leaves behind is the run itself — kept for ninety days when a deployer or an administrator ran it, so it can be viewed, downloaded and compared later; a viewer's replay is answered but not kept — an audit-log entry (policy_whatif, "replayed a draft policy", naming the run) and a counter, attestkeep_policy_whatif_runs_total.

In the console

Policy editor → What if, beside Apply to cluster. Pick a period — 7, 30 or 90 days — and press it. The button is disabled while the form has errors: the draft goes through the same validation a save does, so a draft that cannot be tried is a draft that cannot be applied.

The panel reports three figures — unchanged, would be denied, would be allowed — the gate behind each difference, and up to two hundred of the changed admissions, most recent first. The stored run keeps up to two thousand changed rows and five hundred unscanned digests, and says when either cap cut (samples_capped, unscanned_capped).

Two shortcuts on the policies list: What if on an audit-mode policy opens the editor with the draft switched to enforce and replays the last thirty days immediately; on an enforcing policy it replays the policy as it stands, which surfaces admissions that would be refused today because their scan results have moved. Nothing is applied either way.

If some images in the period have no scan record, the panel says so — "N images have no scan record" — and offers Scan now (deployer and above), which queues scans for exactly those digests, five hundred per request. The replay is not re-run on its own; press What if again once the scans have finished. Until then those rows are decided the way admission treats a cold digest under the draft's admissionTiming.

History and downloads

Security › What-if history lists every run of every policy: when, by whom, the source policy, the period, how many admissions were reviewed and how many would be denied or allowed, and how many images were unscanned. It can be filtered by policy and period. Each run can be viewed again (the same panel, from the stored result), downloaded, or — for an administrator — opened in the editor with Apply this draft, which loads the stored draft without applying it — the diff is then shown against the policy as it is today.

The download is a projection, not evidence. The PDF carries that band on every page and is neither signed nor sealed; a run never enters an evidence package, because the package records what happened and a replay records what would have. The audit log records each download (policy_whatif_download).

GET /api/v1/policies/whatif/runs?policy=<name>&days=<7|30|90>&limit=&offset=
GET /api/v1/policies/whatif/runs/{id}
GET /api/v1/policies/whatif/runs/{id}/download?format=pdf|json
POST /api/v1/images/scan            {"digests": ["sha256:…"]}   # Scan now; at most 500

At most five hundred runs of each kind are kept — policy runs, and deny-list runs apart from them; a new one past that removes the oldest of its kind, and runs older than ninety days are deleted by the operator's hourly sweep. One account may start at most ten replays a minute on each operator replica; the eleventh is answered with 429 and a Retry-After.

The request

POST /api/v1/policies/whatif, session-authenticated. It needs the same authority as reading a policy, not writing one, because it writes nothing.

{
  "policy": { "...": "the same body POST/PUT /api/v1/policies accept" },
  "source": "the policy the draft was edited from (must exist), empty for a new one",
  "days": 30
}

days is 1 to 90 and defaults to 30. An invalid draft is refused with 400 and the exact message Apply would have given.

What the answer contains

FieldWhat it is
kept / run_idWhether the run was stored and, if so, its id; the audit entry and the history page name it. A viewer's replay is not kept.
evaluatedLedger rows the draft was decided against.

contextWhat the draft's deploymentContext gate would touch: its effective mode, how many replayed rows carried each of the six signals (every signal is a key, even at zero), and escalated_denials — refusals that happened only because of where the pod ran. rows_without_context counts rows recorded before migration 0020, which replay with no context at all; above zero it comes with a note saying so, because an empty array cannot tell "carried no signals" from "nobody was looking".

What it covers

  • Rows in the period whose namespace the draft governs. A draft with an empty targetNamespaces is cluster-wide and covers every namespace.
  • Break-glass rows are excluded and counted in break_glass_skipped.
  • Pod-level workload-hardening denials are excluded: they are recorded with no image, and the replay decides images. Reporting them as "unchanged" would be a claim it never checked.
  • The newest 5000 rows.

The caveat that matters: the current scan record

Decisions are replayed against each image's scan record as it stands now, not as it stood at the time. That is what the note on every response says, and it is the one thing that can mislead.

The ledger records what was decided. The scan store records what is known today. An image scanned three times since that admission is judged on today's findings; a CVE published last week counts against a pod admitted last month. This is the right behaviour for the question being asked — "would this policy be safe to turn on" is about the images as they are — but it is not a reconstruction of history, and an evidence package is still the only thing that reports what was actually decided.

Two more things the replay does not reproduce:

  • The licence meter. Metering is a licence fact rather than a policy one, so it is not re-run: replaying it would either double-count the period or make the answer depend on when the button was pressed. A row originally refused for the monthly image allowance therefore shows as "would be allowed", with quota in would_allow_by_reason.
  • Audit-mode masking. A denial recorded under an Audit policy sits in the ledger as allowed, with audit mode: in front of its reason, and the replay compares it against the draft's own decision. That is deliberate, and it is the feature's whole point: a draft that differs from the running policy only in enforcement shows exactly the pods Enforce would have stopped.

An image with no scan record at all is decided the way admission decides a cold digest — by the draft's admissionTiming, or by the installation's webhook.coldImagePolicy when the draft leaves it empty — and lands under unscanned when that refuses it.

Why the answer can be trusted

The replay does not have its own copy of the rules. One function is the whole per-image admission decision — break-glass, the registry allow-list, :latest, digest enforcement, the meter, the cold-image timing, and the severity, known-exploited, provenance and VEX evaluation — and both the admission webhook and this endpoint call it. The preview and the gate cannot drift, because there is only one of them.
